scRNA-seq Fibrosis Dataset Analysis

Abstract

Currently there are no effective antifibrotic therapies for liver cirrhosis, a major killer worldwide. To obtain a cellular resolution of directly-relevant pathogenesis and to inform therapeutic design, we profile the transcriptomes of over 100,000 human single cells, yielding molecular definitions for non-parenchymal cell types present in healthy and cirrhotic human liver. We uncover a novel scar-associated TREM2+CD9+ macrophage subpopulation, which expands in liver fibrosis, differentiates from circulating monocytes and is pro-fibrogenic. We also define novel ACKR1+ and PLVAP+ endothelial cells which expand in cirrhosis, are topographically scar-restricted and enhance leucocyte transmigration. Multi-lineage ligand-receptor modelling of interactions between the novel scar-associated macrophages, endothelial cells and PDGFRα+ collagen-producing mesenchymal cells reveals intra-scar activity of several pro-fibrogenic pathways including TNFRSF12A, PDGFR and NOTCH signalling. Our work dissects unanticipated aspects of the cellular and molecular basis of human organ fibrosis at a single-cell level, and provides the conceptual framework required to discover rational therapeutic targets in liver cirrhosis.

Dataset Design

Single-cell transcriptomic data were analyzed from 5 healthy livers, 5 cirrhotic livers, and 4 PBMC samples from cirrhotic patients. In parallel, single-cell transcriptomic data were also evaluated on hepatic macrophages isolated from healthy mice and from mice following chronic carbon tetrachloride administration to model the disease.

Analysis Design

The primary objective of this study was to isolate reliable target genes in the human cirrhotic liver using scRNA-seq data. 1) Data retrieval from the provided accession number GSE136103 and creation of Seurat object was first performed to establish the computational framework. 2) QC of the scRNA-seq using the “Seurat” package was then conducted to filter out low-quality cells, followed immediately by 3) Batch Correction and Integration to eliminate technical variation. 4) Dimension Reduction was executed via PCA and UMAP/t-SNE for 2D mapping, enabling the 5) Visualization of Markers associated with the data and annotation of celltypes. 6) Assess which of celltypes are more profound in cirrhotic tissue vs healthy tissue.

To understand transcriptomic shifts, the pipeline was designed to 7) Conduct Gene Differentials for each celltype and overall for all cells, followed by 8) Finding Gene Ontology Terms and Building GO terms plots to resolve functional biological insights. Given their critical roles in liver pathology, specialized sub-analyses were performed, including 9) Endothelial subcluster annotation and analysis, 10) Mesenchymal subcluster annotation and analysis, and 11) Macrophage subcluster annotation and analysis. A 12) Random forest implementation to select for features that classify cirrhosis cells and healthy cells accurately on Endothelial, Mesenchyme and MP cluster was then utilized to leverage machine learning for biomarker discovery. This culminated in the 13) Identificaton of features that are improtant and up-regulated via DEGs to yield a refined, high-confidence set of prioritized targets for liver cirrhosis.

Part 2: Sample Quality Control/Pre-processing

To refine the dataset, cells with fewer than 300 detected genes (nFeature_RNA) or a mitochondrial gene percentage exceeding 30% will be removed to eliminate low-quality or dying cells. Conversely, an upper threshold will be applied to exclude cells with more than 4,000 detected genes, targeting potential multiplets.

Following these manual thresholds, DoubletFinder will be implemented to computationally identify and remove remaining doublets. Based on community benchmarks and empirical data discussed on relevant GitHub repositories for this technology, an expected doublet rate of 3% will be utilized based on the typical cell density per sample in this dataset.

Part 3: Batch Correction & Integration of Samples

Seurat integration steps do batch correction thus we do not need to take any steps. I used the first sample as reference to do the integration.

Part 4: Dimensional Reduction, Clustering

We have scaled the batch corrected counts, run principal components analysis and used harmony which is notorious for robust clustering. We calculated neighbors based on 15 harmony dimensions and clusters were calculated at 0.4. Finally, a UMAP was created based on the harmony dimensions.

# 3. Data Splitting & Resampling
set.seed(123) # Recommended for reproducibility
endothelial_split <- initial_split(endothelial_scaled_df, strata = "cell_type")
endothelial_train <- training(endothelial_split)
endothelial_test  <- testing(endothelial_split)

# 10-fold cross-validation
endothelial_folds <- vfold_cv(endothelial_train, v = 10)


# 4. Recipe Configuration (Preprocessing)
endothelial_recipe <- recipe(formula = cell_type ~ ., data = endothelial_train) %>%
  update_role(cell_barcode, new_role = "ID") %>%
  step_zv(all_predictors())


# 5. Model Specification & Workflow Pipeline
endothelial_spec <- rand_forest() %>%
  set_engine("randomForest", importance = TRUE) %>%
  set_mode("classification")

endothelial_workflow <- workflow() %>% 
  add_recipe(endothelial_recipe) %>% 
  add_model(endothelial_spec)


# 6. Model Training
endothelial_fit <- fit(endothelial_workflow, data = endothelial_train)


# 7. Model Evaluation (Confusion Matrix)
